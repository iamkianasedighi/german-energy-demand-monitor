German Electricity Market Monitor

When does electricity become cheaper on the day-ahead market, and what do wind, solar and residual demand have to do with it? I joined one year of SMARD load and generation data with wholesale prices to investigate.

Prices and residual demand move together

All 462 negative-price hours fell below the year's 25th-percentile residual-load threshold (16.9 GW). Price and residual load also had a Spearman rank correlation of 0.69. The comparison describes the market, not a causal effect.

What the time-of-day comparison shows

On 90% of the 365 days with prices at both hours, 20:00 was more expensive than 13:00. The median difference was about €116/MWh. These hours have the highest and lowest median prices across clock hours in the observed year.

For a company with shiftable electricity use, this could justify checking its contract and operational constraints. It is not a calculation of achievable cost savings: wholesale prices do not equal the price paid by every customer.

Summer midday stands apart

At 14:00 in summer, 75% of observations were in the year's bottom 10% of residual load, versus roughly 3% in winter. This seasonal heatmap uses a stricter threshold (3.5 GW) than the price comparison; the two cutoffs answer different questions.
